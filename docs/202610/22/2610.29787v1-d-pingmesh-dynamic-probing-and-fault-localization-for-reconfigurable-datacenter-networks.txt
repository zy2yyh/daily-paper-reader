## Abstract 

Pingmesh monitors datacenter networks through periodic pings between servers. In reconfigurable datacenter net-works, topology engineering (TPE) and traffic engineering (TE) make uniform server-pair probing inefficient: a pinglist wastes budget retesting common paths while missing low-probability interfaces. Frequent control-plane updates fur-ther require rapid pinglist refresh, and cause path ambigu-ity at control-plane boundaries for fault localization. We present D-Pingmesh, a probing and fault-localization system co-designed with the TPE/TE control plane. Its control-plane-aware generator builds task-efficient pinglists by greedily admitting probes with high interface coverage under the current TPE/TE snapshot. An incremental replay-and-repair mechanism updates pinglists across reconfigurations rather than recomputing from scratch. At control-plane bound-aries, a Rho-EM localizer jointly infers per-task old/new-path mixture weights and the fault set through EM-style iteration. We evaluate D-Pingmesh on an 8-pod reconfig-urable topology under dynamic TPE/TE. At a 0.99 coverage target, D-Pingmesh uses 17.7 × fewer tasks than Pingmesh and generates each pinglist about 5.1 × faster than RD-Probe. In ns-3 simulation, boundary-aware localization improves micro F1 from 0.871 to 0.953 over the deTector PLL baseline. A testbed deployment demonstrates second-scale fault local-ization with low host overhead. These results demonstrate that D-Pingmesh is practical for reconfigurable datacenter networks with control-plane-aware monitoring.

## 1 Introduction 

Network faults remain a critical source of availability inci-dents in modern datacenters. Even when per-component failure rates appear small, they become operationally sig-nificant at cluster scale: measurements of Google’s TPUv4 deployment report average daily failure rates of 0.005% for interconnect cables and 0.04% for optical circuit switches (OCSes) [37]. In clusters with thousands to tens of thou-sands of devices, such rates make online network testing and accurate fault localization necessary rather than optional. Active probing remains one of the most practical mech-anisms for this purpose. By sending probe traffic between servers, it provides direct end-to-end evidence about reacha-bility and latency without requiring pervasive switch instru-mentation or changes to production applications. Pingmesh established this model for large datacenter networks [11]. Subsequent systems have improved probe placement, cover-age guarantees, service awareness, and fault localization for different settings [9, 12, 21, 24, 30]. These systems, however, largely assume that the network state used to plan probes remains stable within a measurement window: once a task is selected, its observations can be associated with a small set of expected paths. This assumption breaks down in modern datacenters, which increasingly treat the network as a reconfigurable resource driven by two architectural control-plane mechanisms. Topol-ogy engineering (TPE) reconfigures the OCS to align con-nectivity with traffic demand [3, 5, 16, 20, 25, 29]. Traffic engineering (TE) adjusts weighted forwarding splits over that topology to accommodate shorter-term demand fluc-tuations [22, 23, 33]. These mechanisms can change which physical interfaces a given packet traverses at sub-minute to minute timescales [23]. AI training and inference clusters further reinforce this trend: their recurring communication patterns make TPE and TE especially effective at adapting the network to demand [14, 31].

However, probing systems such as Pingmesh were de-signed and have evolved independently of these control loops. They treat the network as a static underlay and plan probes in terms of server pairs, without consuming the current TPE/TE state as input. Consider a TE update that changes the forwarding weights between two pods: a probe task between the same two servers can resolve to different interfaces before and after the update. A monitor seeing only the server pair cannot determine whether a loss observation belongs to the old path or the new one. Across our measure-ment traces, 15–28% of probe tasks change their resolved path across a single TE update on average. The server pair, which traditional probing uses as the stable unit of diagnosis, loses its anchoring power once TE remaps the same pair to different interfaces. A single timely detection may suffice for a persistent hard failure, but diagnosing gray packet loss requires repeated observations, and transient faults may disappear before the next stable window. These observation requirements mo-tivate continuous probing that gathers sufficient evidence while faults are present and correctly interprets observations across reconfigurations. Meeting these requirements calls for co-designing net-work monitoring with the TPE/TE control plane to overcome three challenges. First, TPE and TE induce asymmetry in interface hit probability. Under uniform server-pair selection, probes concentrate on high-probability interfaces and may starve others in the same network state. Second, TPE/TE updates introduce dynamics : a pinglist optimized for one snapshot may lose coverage after the next update, yet re-generating from scratch discards the coverage contribution of tasks that remain valid. Third, TPE/TE transitions create 

boundary inconsistency for diagnosis. Because TE updates deploy at pod granularity rather than atomically, different parts of the network transiently operate under different TE snapshots, so probe observations in the same probe window may correspond to different path states. To address these challenges, we present D-Pingmesh, a probing and fault-localization system co-designed with the TPE/TE control plane. D-Pingmesh consumes current topol-ogy and TPE/TE snapshots, and uses reconfiguration timing to schedule probe windows at ping agents. For each candi-date ping task, D-Pingmesh determines the interfaces that the task can observe under the current control-plane state and uses this information for both task planning and fault interpretation. D-Pingmesh tackles the asymmetry challenge by formu-lating probe generation as an interface multicover problem under the current control-plane state. It scores pod pairs by their expected coverage contribution, samples candidate five-tuples from high-scoring pairs, and greedily admits tasks that advance the multicover objective. ## 2 Background and Motivation 

2.1 Reconfigurable Datacenter Networks 

We focus on reconfigurable datacenter networks that com-bine an electrical intra-pod hierarchy with optical inter-pod connectivity, as sketched in Figure 1(a). Within each pod, traffic is forwarded through servers, Top-of-Rack switches (ToRs), and aggregation (Agg) switches. Across pods, OC-Ses connect Agg uplinks to remote Agg ports, replacing the fixed electrical spine of a conventional Clos network. Such OCS-based designs appear in Google’s Jupiter Evolving [25] and TROD [5]. Operating such a network requires two coupled control-plane decisions: TPE, which establishes optical circuits, and TE, which splits traffic over the resulting connectivity. Both are driven by traffic demand to improve network utilization under changing workloads [5, 25, 33]. Measurements from production datacenters confirm that inter-server traffic ex-hibits significant locality and stability [28], supporting the feasibility of demand-driven reconfiguration. TPE changes inter-pod optical connectivity by program-ming the OCS’s internal cross-connects, while the physical Agg-to-OCS uplinks remain fixed. These cross-connects pair selected OCS ports to form duplex optical circuits, as shown in Figure 1(b) [25]. TE determines how traffic is split over the resulting con-nectivity, installing weighted next-hop groups at ToRs and Aggs. Recent TE systems compute these weights to bal-ance load and improve utilization under dynamic traffic de-mand [22, 23, 33]. TE can operate on short timescales: recent work reports split-ratio adjustment periods from 10 seconds to 15 minutes [23]. These weighted groups can be realized via weighted-cost multipath (WCMP), where a switch hashes a five-tuple (source/destination IP, ports, and protocol) to one member of the weighted group [36]. Consequently, two packets between the same server pair may traverse different interfaces. Direct OCS links match persistent traffic trends, but direct-only forwarding cannot absorb bursty deviations. Existing designs therefore use TE to steer selected traffic over one-hop detours through intermediate pods [5, 25]. We call a path direct when it uses an active optical circuit toward the destination pod, and a detour when it passes through an intermediate pod before reaching the destination. From a monitoring perspective, detours increase path uncertainty: the same server pair may resolve to paths with different sets of switch interfaces depending on whether the probe takes a direct or detour route. Together, TPE and TE introduce within-snapshot asymme-try and across-snapshot dynamics, challenging the uniform, stable forwarding assumptions of conventional Pingmesh (Section 2.3). 

2.2 Limitations of Prior Monitoring Systems 

Prior systems have made substantial progress in datacenter monitoring and fault localization. Pingmesh [11] demon-strates the feasibility of always-on, server-based measure-ment at production scale, but its uniform server-pair probing spends a large task budget to obtain broad visibility. deTec-tor [24] reduces probing overhead by selecting a topology-aware probe matrix that jointly considers coverage and loss localization, but computing such matrices becomes expen-sive at large scale. RD-Probe [9] further targets sufficient Layer-2 coverage in complex production networks by com-bining randomized probing with deterministic repair, but its randomized phase can still spend probes on already well-covered components before the repair phase fills remain-ing gaps. These systems are not designed around TPE and TE awareness, limiting their ability to generate and update pinglists efficiently and to localize faults accurately in dy-namic reconfigurable networks. Systems for explicit path control or path tracking, such as NetBouncer [30] and Byte-Tracker [12], are not directly applicable in OCS-based re-configurable fabrics. We treat them as complementary path-visibility mechanisms rather than direct baselines. Production reports also illustrate the cost of increasing uni-form probing. Microsoft’s IMC’18 study reports that Pingmesh limits full-mesh measurements to servers within the same rack because of scaling concerns [27]. RD-Probe reports that, in the production datacenter it studied, increasing a prior Pingmesh-like system from three to four probe tasks per server pair would overload some agents [9]. 2.3 Challenges Challenge 1: Topology and Forwarding Asymmetry. Re-configurable datacenter networks introduce both topology asymmetry and forwarding asymmetry. Topology asymme-try comes from TPE, which determines which cross-pod optical links are available in the current snapshot, so direct connectivity can be uneven across pod pairs. Forwarding asymmetry comes from TE on top of this topology: TE in-stalls weighted next-hop groups and may split traffic between direct and one-hop detour paths. As a result, interface hit probabilities can be highly skewed: some interfaces lie on high-probability forwarding paths, while interfaces on low-weight next hops may be traversed by only a small fraction of candidate probes. Endpoint-uniform probing then cre-ates redundant observations on high-probability interfaces, while low-probability interfaces may fail to reach the target coverage count needed for diagnosis. Figure 2 illustrates this effect with a toy forwarding state. Traffic from pod 0 to pod 1 can either use Agg 0 to reach pod 1 directly, or use Agg 1 to reach pod 2 and then take a one-hop detour to pod 1. The ToR-to-Agg forwarding split is 7:3: Agg 0 leads to the direct path, while Agg 1 leads to the one-hop detour path. Challenge 2: Topology and Forwarding Dynamics. After a TPE or TE update, a pinglist optimized for the prior snap-shot may lose coverage: the same five-tuple can resolve to different interfaces under the new state. TE updates occur on timescales of 10 seconds to 15 minutes [23], so recomputing the full pinglist from scratch after each change is expensive. Yet simply reusing the old pinglist leaves newly exposed or remapped interfaces uncovered. The challenge is to update the pinglist incrementally while preserving still-valid tasks, repairing coverage deficits, and pruning redundancy with low overhead. 

Challenge 3: Boundary Inconsistency. Traditional data-center probing systems often operate under a comparatively stable topology and forwarding model. In that setting, a probe degradation signal can be interpreted against a rela-tively well-defined forwarding path. Reconfigurable datacen-ter networks break this assumption. As shown in Figure 3, ping agents collect repeated probe results over each probe window, such as 30 seconds, and report the aggregate to the controller for analysis and fault localization, rather than 

> 4

reporting each probe independently. Although a 30-second probe window is short, it can occupy a significant fraction of a TE window when TE is updated within 15 minutes [23]. Moreover, TE decisions may be deployed at pod granularity rather than as a single global update, as in Google’s Jupiter Evolving [25]. Around a TE boundary, the resulting global ob-servation is therefore a mixture of multiple local forwarding states rather than a snapshot of one consistent network-wide state. We refer to this mismatch as boundary inconsistency. Boundary-window observations can be the only evidence of a transient fault. Among 1,463 link flaps recorded over 30 days in our production dataset, 96.4% recovered within 30 seconds (Figure 4), the example probe-window duration in Figure 3. Waiting for a stable window can therefore miss the fault. Continuous probing can retain and correctly inter-pret observations collected across the boundary. Figure 5 quantifies the effect: across TE boundaries in three represen-tative scenarios (S1–S3), 15.25–28.29% of tasks change their resolved paths on average, and fault localization F1 drops from 95.3% to 88.9% under a TE-boundary window compared to steady state. These measurements require careful inter-pretation: if analyzed only with the latest forwarding state, the localizer can assign loss evidence to the wrong candidate interfaces. 

## 3 System Overview 

Figure 6 gives an overview of D-Pingmesh and its control-plane-aware probing and diagnosis loop. The Software-Defined Networking (SDN) control plane computes TPE and TE deci-sions from traffic demand and installs TPE configurations in the optical circuit switched core and TE weighted next-hop groups in the electrical switching hierarchy. D-Pingmesh runs alongside this control plane and, for each reconfigu-ration slot, receives the current TPE snapshot, TE forward-ing state, and the schedule of upcoming TPE/TE updates. It also receives static inputs, including the network topol-ogy and the WCMP hash functions used by the switches. With this control-plane awareness, the D-Pingmesh con-troller generates slot-specific pinglists, aligns probe windows with TPE/TE updates, and localizes faulty interfaces from aggregate probe results. Ping agents on host servers execute controller-computed pinglists in synchronized windows and return aggregate attempt and failure counts. Scheduling, in-cremental replay and repair, and localization reside at the controller; D-Pingmesh requires no switch modifications.

Control-plane-aware probe generation. D-Pingmesh’s probe generator uses the current TPE and TE state to produce a probe-efficient task list for each probe window. We formu-late this as an interface multicover problem under skewed interface hit probabilities (§2.3): each candidate five-tuple covers its resolved interfaces, and the objective is to meet each target interface’s coverage requirement with as few tasks as possible. D-Pingmesh uses a probability-guided al-gorithm that iteratively selects the pod pair with the highest expected coverage gain, generates candidate tasks, and uses hash-aware path resolution to accept only those that improve coverage. §4.2 and §4.3 present the multicover formulation and the probability-guided generation algorithm. 

Dynamic incremental probe update. TPE and TE updates can change the resolved paths of existing probe tasks and the interfaces they cover. Regenerating the full pinglist after every update wastes the coverage already provided by tasks that remain useful under the new snapshot. D-Pingmesh therefore maintains the pinglist incrementally: it re-resolves the previous list under the new snapshot, repairs the remain-ing coverage deficits, and removes redundancy from the merged list. The controller distributes the updated pinglist to ping agents and synchronizes probe windows with the TPE/TE update cycle. §4.4 details the incremental update algorithm. 

Boundary-aware fault localization. To handle the bound-ary uncertainty introduced by frequent TPE and TE updates (§2.3), we design Rho-EM, a boundary-aware fault localiza-tion algorithm. Rho-EM operates on aggregate probe results from each probe window. When a probe window contains a control-plane update boundary, the same logical probe task may correspond to either the pre-update path or the post-update path, while the aggregate result does not ex-pose the per-attempt path state. For each task, the algorithm resolves both path hypotheses and maintains a per-task mix-ture over the old and new paths. It scores candidate faulty

interfaces using a likelihood over aggregate probe counts and jointly refines the fault set and per-task path mixtures through EM-style iteration. §5 details the likelihood model and the Rho-EM algorithm. 

## 4 Probe Generation and Update with Control-Plane Awareness 

This section addresses the first two challenges from §2.3. D-Pingmesh first obtains the current TPE/TE snapshot and uses hash-aware path resolution to map candidate five-tuples to their link-level paths (§4.1). The probe generator then se-lects probe tasks that improve interface multicover under that snapshot (§4.3). To handle TPE/TE dynamics, we de-sign an incremental probe update algorithm that re-resolves the previous pinglist under the new snapshot, repairs resid-ual coverage deficits, and prunes redundant tasks without reducing achieved coverage (§4.4).

## 5 Fault Localization Across Reconfiguration Boundaries 

Probe generation selects which tasks to measure, while fault localization inverts the resulting aggregate observations into a ranked list of likely faulty interfaces. Traditional datacen-ter networks map each probe to a deterministic path, but reconfigurable networks break this assumption: around a TPE or TE update, the same task can traverse different paths, and an observation near a reconfiguration boundary could belong to either the pre- or post-update state. This ambiguity arises because TE updates deploy at pod granularity rather than atomically across the network. D-Pingmesh addresses this with Rho-EM, a boundary-aware localizer that maintains both pre- and post-update path hypotheses for each task. Rho-EM treats the per-attempt path as a hidden variable and jointly refines per-task mix-ture weights and the fault set through EM-style iteration. It consumes only aggregate success and failure counts per task within each probe window together with the corresponding control-plane snapshots, and returns a predicted fault set with a ranked suspicion list.

12 7 Related Work 

Datacenter active probing and diagnosis. Pingmesh [11] established production-scale active probing for datacenter monitoring. R-Pingmesh [21] extended this to RoCE net-works with service-aware probing and in-network drop dif-ferentiation. For fault diagnosis, 007 [2] aggregates end-host observations to localize packet-drop faults, NetBouncer [30] uses IP-in-IP bouncing to probe designated paths, and Byte-Tracker [12] tracks probe paths with centralized probers and switch mirroring. deTector [24] selects topology-aware probe matrices for joint coverage and loss localization, and RD-Probe [9] combines randomized and deterministic gen-eration to guarantee L2 coverage at scale. These earlier sys-tems assume stable forwarding paths. OmniPath Ping [32] uses switch-host co-design for service tracing under packet spraying, whereas D-Pingmesh targets path changes across TPE/TE reconfigurations. 

Runtime forwarding verification. VeriDP [34] moni-tors consistency between control-plane policies and actual packet forwarding, while Hydra [26] verifies packet pro-cessing against specifications at runtime. Both complement D-Pingmesh’s probing and fault localization. 

Traffic engineering. Recent work on precise datacenter TE [7] studied realizing desired weight distributions under constrained hardware resources. Gemini [33] jointly opti-mizes inter-pod topology and routing for reconfigurable datacenter networks. FIGRET [22] enhances TE robustness against traffic bursts with fine-grained per-source-destination-pair protection. SSDO [23] accelerates large-scale TE via a sequential solver-free algorithm. We use TE output as the forwarding state for probe generation and fault localization. 

Reconfigurable datacenter networks. OSA [6] established the case for optical reconfiguration, while Sirius [3] demon-strated a flat fabric with nanosecond switching. Jupiter Evolv-ing [25] and Lightwave [20] demonstrated production OCS fabrics. TPUv4 [13] deployed OCS to reconfigure machine-learning interconnects, while InfiniteHBD [29] uses OCS transceivers to build high-bandwidth domains for LLM train-ing. TROD [4, 5] co-designs threshold-based routing and topology for optical datacenters. NegotiaToR [19] uses dis-tributed on-demand scheduling. OpenOptics [16] provides an open framework for optical architectures, and SyncWise [17] studies time synchronization for reconfigurable networks. 

## 8 Discussion 

Generality. D-Pingmesh formulates probe generation and fault localization over the abstractions of topology, TE weights, and WCMP hash behavior, without relying on properties spe-cific to OCS hardware. The approach applies to any network in which forwarding paths change on timescales shorter than the measurement window, including Low Earth Or-bit (LEO) satellite constellations, where inter-satellite links reconfigure under orbital motion [15, 18]. 

Fault model and observation granularity. Rho-EM’s like-lihood model assumes per-interface independent loss with a stable rate within one probe window. The model does not capture failures with shared-risk structure, such as an OCS port affecting multiple circuits, nor time-varying loss rates. Task-level 𝜌 𝑞 accommodates heterogeneous old/new path mixtures using the existing aggregate counters. A per-pod commit-time model uses fewer parameters, but ties the mixtures to a shared transition time within each pod. Sub-window reporting divides a probe window into shorter inter-vals and reports per-task attempt and failure counts for each interval. This provides finer temporal evidence, but increases reporting volume and reduces samples per interval at a fixed probing rate. 

Deployment trade-off. D-Pingmesh preserves deployment simplicity: agents execute pinglists and report aggregate counters, while switches remain unchanged. Additional com-putation and state reside at the controller. D-Pingmesh suits large, frequently reconfigured fabrics; Pingmesh-style mon-itoring remains simpler for small, mostly static networks when probing and reporting costs are acceptable. 

Towards faster TE cadences. Our evaluation uses 15-minute TE windows [23]. At the 8-pod scale, D-Pingmesh averages 2.462 s/slot for probe generation (Figure 7). Faster TE ca-dences may require shortening the 30-second observation window, reducing probe attempts per interface. Raising the per-interface coverage target 𝑘 could help compensate, at the cost of additional probing. 

More frequent TPE reconfiguration. If TPE reconfigu-ration moves to hourly or minute-level operation, changes in optical connectivity would create more frequent pinglist invalidation and boundary ambiguity, increasing the impor-tance of Challenges 2 and 3. Our controlled TPE-transition experiment tests incremental generation after the new state is available; handling observations during optical switching requires further evaluation. 

## 9 Conclusion 

D-Pingmesh co-designs probing and fault localization with the TPE/TE control plane for reconfigurable datacenter net-works. It combines probability-guided probe generation, in-cremental pinglist updates, and Rho-EM, which jointly re-fines per-task old/new-path mixtures and the fault set to resolve boundary ambiguity. Compared with prior systems, D-Pingmesh achieves equivalent coverage with fewer tasks and lower generation time, while improving fault localiza-tion under continuous reconfiguration.
