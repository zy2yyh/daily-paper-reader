# An Information-Geometric Framework for OCS-Reconfigurable Interconnect Topology in Large-Scale AI Clusters 

Shintaro Yoshizawa

Abstract 

The interconnects of large AI training clusters, exemplified by NVIDIA’s GB200 NVL72 systems, are built from static and symmetric fabrics: an NVLink domain within the rack and a Fat-Tree between racks. Optical circuit switches render the inter-pod wiring programmable, and thereby raise three design questions that static fabrics never pose: which wiring to deploy, how far it should depart from the symmetric reference, and when it should be changed. This paper answers them within a single information-geometric framework. A wiring is a point of the probability simplex over candidate circuits, constrained to a degree polytope, and its departure from a symmetric prior encoding the uniform logical capacity of a non-blocking Fat-Tree is measured by the Kullback–Leibler divergence. The cost is a smoothed critical-path length of the training-iteration DAG; it is convex with explicit smoothness constants, and its gradient is given by the marginals of a Gibbs distribution over DAG paths, computable by a forward–backward recursion. For the free energy Fβ1 = U +

β−11 DKL (· ∥ q) we establish well-posedness, monotone dependence on the inverse temperature 

β1 with quantitative limits at both ends, a lower bound on the gain of adaptation by the Jeffreys divergence between the static and the adaptive wiring, and a symmetry principle that characterises exactly when static wiring is optimal. The associated entropic proximal-gradient method reduces each step to a multiplicative update followed by a KL projection with a |V |-dimensional dual, and converges linearly in KL divergence, also in the presence of inexact projections. For time-varying traffic, a threshold policy attains regret O(√ΓCV 1T ) against the switching-free dynamic optimum, with a finite-sample counterpart whose radius depends on the support size rather than the ambient dimension; the KL indicator of traffic change is identified as the multinomial generalised likelihood-ratio statistic, which yields an adaptive-lag GLR–CUSUM detector with a non-asymptotic false-alarm bound. For clusters shared by several jobs, generalised-mean fairness of slowdowns is convex, coincides in its maximisers with α-fairness of throughputs for α ≥ 2, and leads to a Gibbs-form optimality condition. Synthetic experiments on pipeline-, data- and expert-parallel workloads confirm the theory and delineate its scope, including the regime in which exact integer programming remains preferable.

11 Introduction 

1.1 The Communication Bottleneck in LLM Training 

Large language models (LLMs) now reach hundreds of billions to trillions of parameters [6, 7, 28], and training them requires tens of thousands of accelerators operating in concert. The inter-connect is a first-order determinant of throughput and of reliability: during a 54-day snapshot of Llama 3 405B pre-training, 35 of 419 unexpected interruptions (8.4%) were attributed to network switches and cables [18]. A training job is decomposed along tensor (TP), pipeline (PP), data (DP) and, for mixture-of-experts (MoE) models, expert (EP) parallelism [12, 24]. Each dimension induces a distinct collective: TP issues latency-critical collectives that normally stay inside the scale-up domain, PP sends activations and gradients between adjacent stages, DP performs gradient all-reduce, and EP performs all-to-all exchanges whose volume depends on token routing. The resulting traffic is strongly structured, phase-dependent, and—for MoE—time-varying. 

1.2 Fat-Tree as the Dominant Paradigm and Its Limitations 

The k-ary Fat-Tree [1, 15] is the de facto standard for HPC and AI clusters. NVIDIA’s DGX SuperPOD reference architecture uses a multi-tier InfiniBand Fat-Tree [20]; the Blackwell gen-eration adds a rack-scale NVLink domain (GB200 NVL72: 72 GPUs, fifth-generation NVLink, 1.8 TB/s per GPU and 130 TB/s in aggregate) with an InfiniBand or Ethernet Fat-Tree be-tween racks [21]. A non-blocking Fat-Tree offers the same logical capacity to every host pair and is therefore optimal for worst-case (uniform) traffic. For structured LLM traffic it is over-provisioned in some directions and cannot be re-shaped when the parallelism layout changes: the only remaining degree of freedom is routing, constrained by fixed wiring. 

1.3 Optical Circuit Switching: Promise and Open Problems 

Optical circuit switches establish reconfigurable point-to-point light paths that bypass electrical packet switching. Google’s TPU v4 [14] interconnects 4,096 chips through a 3D torus whose slices are composed per job by 48 OCS units, and Google’s data-centre networks use OCS at scale [23, 29]. Topology engineering for such fabrics is typically posed as an integer or mixed-integer programme: COUDER [27] optimises over sets of traffic matrices and reports, on Facebook traces, about 20% higher throughput and 32% lower average hop count than cost-equivalent static topologies; ATRO [13] jointly optimises topology and routing, finding the global optimum for one-hop routing and alternating updates for multi-hop routing; TopoOpt [30] co-optimises topology and parallelisation for training jobs; DELTA [11] encodes the training DAG in a MILP and reports up to 17.5% lower communication time and at least 20% fewer optical ports than traffic-matrix baselines; and InfiniteHBD [25] builds a data-centre-scale high-bandwidth domain from OCS transceivers, reporting a cost of 31% of NVL72. What is missing is a continuous 

theory that (a) states what a reasonable departure from the symmetric Fat-Tree reference is, (b) admits provable, low-complexity updates, (c) incorporates the causal structure of the training DAG, and (d) gives a statistically principled rule for when to reconfigure. 

1.4 Contributions and Their Relation to Known Results 

We develop such a theory by combining elementary information geometry [2, 3, 9] with first-order methods in Bregman geometry [4, 17] and classical sequential analysis [16, 26]. The tools are standard; the contribution lies in the formulation they make possible and in the results specific to it. We regard the following three as the principal contributions. (I) A temperature-controlled design principle (Section 4). Wirings are points of a simplex restricted to a degree polytope, and their departure from a symmetric Fat-Tree-2like prior is penalised by the KL divergence with weight β−11 . We prove well-posedness and strict positivity (Theorem 4.2), monotone and quantitative limits in β1 (Theorem 4.3), a lower bound on the gain of adaptation by the Jeffreys divergence between static and adaptive wirings (Theorem 4.4), and a symmetry principle identifying exactly when static wiring is optimal (Theorems 4.5 and 4.7). (II) A differentiable DAG cost (Section 3). The iteration time is a smoothed critical path of the training DAG; it is convex with explicit smoothness constants (Theorem 3.3), and its gradient is the vector of path criticalities of a Gibbs distribution over DAG paths, computed by a forward–backward recursion (Theorem 3.4). (III) Guarantees for reconfiguration (Section 6). A threshold policy has regret 

O(√ΓCV 1T ) against the switching-free dynamic optimum (Theorem 6.3), with a KL-threshold variant (Theorem 6.5) and a finite-sample version whose radius scales with the support size (Theorem 6.7); the GLR–CUSUM detector obtained from the likelihood-ratio form of the KL indicator controls false alarms (Theorem 6.9). Two further components apply established results to the present model and are included to make the framework complete and verifiable: the convergence analysis of the entropic proximal-gradient method (Section 5; Theorems 5.4 and 5.5), which specialises the theory of Bregman proximal gradient methods under relative smoothness, and the multi-tenant extension (Sec-tion 7), which combines the KL chain rule with generalised-mean fairness and identifies the range 

α ≥ 2 in which fairness and convexity are compatible (Theorems 7.2, 7.4 and 7.5). Synthetic experiments (Section 8) verify every bound and compare against static, demand-proportional and exact integer baselines. We have not carried out an exhaustive priority search, and make no claim that the individual inequalities are new as abstract statements; their interest lies in what they assert about reconfigurable fabrics. 

# 2 Background 

2.1 Fat-Tree, Blackwell, and OCS Fabrics 

A k-ary Fat-Tree has ( k/ 2) 2 core switches and k pods, each with k/ 2 aggregation and k/ 2edge switches; every edge switch serves k/ 2 hosts, for k3/4 hosts and k3/2 inter-switch links in total. Its automorphism group permutes pods and the hosts below an edge switch, and with full bisection bandwidth ( k3/4)( c/ 2) it offers the same logical capacity to every host pair. Figure 1(a) shows k = 4. In Blackwell NVL72 systems [21] the 72 GPUs of a rack are connected through NVSwitch trays; each GPU also has a NIC to the scale-out InfiniBand or Ethernet Fat-Tree (Figure 1(b)). All layers are static. An OCS device establishes point-to-point optical circuits between transceivers; switching takes milliseconds for MEMS devices, while end-to-end reconfiguration including transceiver and NIC re-initialisation is reported in the range of seconds [11, 22]. We work at pod granularity: a pod is a scale-up domain (e.g. a rack) with r OCS uplinks; the OCS layer realises any multigraph on the pods in which every pod has degree at most r and the total number of circuits does not exceed the OCS port budget μ (Figure 1(c)).

3.2 The Symmetric Prior 

The reference wiring is a positive q ∈ ∆◦. Our default is the uniform distribution on E: at pod granularity this is the logical capacity a non-blocking Fat-Tree offers to uniform traffic, namely an equal share for every pod pair, and it is invariant under all permutations of pods. We write qW for its KL projection (1) onto W; when q ∈ W (as for the uniform prior under Assumption A1 with complete E), qW = q.

Remark 3.2 (Priors with restricted support) . If one wishes to use a prior supported on a subset E0 ⊊ E (for instance the links of a physical Fat-Tree), then DKL (w ∥ q) = + ∞ for every wiring that puts mass outside E0, so the penalty forbids any departure from the support. The smoothed prior qϵ = (1 − ϵ) Unif( E0) + ϵ Unif( E \ E0), ϵ ∈ (0 , 1), is positive and all results below apply to it; the bounds depend on ϵ through DKL (· ∥ qϵ) ≤ log( |E|/ϵ ).

Remark 3.5 (The DAG as an exponential family) . Phase durations are consequences of the wiring rather than free decision variables, so precedence constraints are not imposed on a separate schedule; they enter the problem exactly through the critical-path functional J. The geometric object that the DAG induces is the exponential family {στ } on P, with sufficient statistic the path length and natural parameter 1 /τ ; the criticalities γv are its marginals, and the forward– backward recursion is the familiar sum–product computation of these marginals.

# 9 Discussion 

What the geometry contributes. The information-geometric structure is used for three concrete purposes. (i) The KL penalty to a symmetric prior gives a convex, strictly positive and symmetry-respecting interpolation between static and adaptive wirings whose two ends are char-acterised exactly (Theorem 4.3), and the Pythagorean inequality turns this into a quantitative gain bound (Theorem 4.4). (ii) Entropic Bregman geometry makes the proximal step a multi-plicative update followed by a KL projection with a |V |-dimensional dual, and the three-point property delivers linear convergence without Euclidean projections onto the simplex. (iii) Ex-ponential families appear twice more: the Gibbs distribution over DAG paths whose marginals 22 are the criticalities, and the multinomial likelihood whose GLR statistic is the KL indicator. 

Limitations. 

• Model. Routing is single-hop on direct circuits in parallel with a static fabric, and con-current phases are assumed not to contend (Assumption A2). Multi-hop routing (as in ATRO [13]) would add a flow block coupled to the wiring through capacity constraints; the joint convexity of perspective functions κ ϕ (f /κ ) makes this a natural extension, but its analysis is not done here. 

• Integrality. The theory concerns the continuous relaxation. On our instances an exact MILP solved by HiGHS is fast and, for few ports per pod, clearly better than rounding plus local search (Section 8.3); the continuous method is competitive when the number of ports per pod is large, where Theorem 5.7 predicts small rounding loss. We therefore do not claim that the method replaces integer programming. 

• Constants. L in Theorem 3.3 and Γ in Theorem 6.1 are worst-case and conservative; backtracking avoids needing L, but the regret bounds are loose in absolute terms and should be read as scaling laws. 

• Statistics. The false-alarm bound grows with the number of cells M ; pooling cells (e.g. by pod pair) reduces M at the cost of resolution. The effect of estimating the reference distribution is not analysed. 

• Evaluation. All experiments are synthetic. Validation on production traces and on real OCS hardware, including the cost and duration of reconfiguration, is required. 

# 10 Conclusion 

Optical circuit switching turns the wiring of an AI cluster from a fixed datum into a decision variable. We have argued that the appropriate language for this decision is the geometry of the probability simplex. In that language a single inverse temperature measures how far a deployment departs from the symmetric Fat-Tree reference; the limits of this departure, the gain it can buy and the circumstances in which it buys nothing admit exact statements; the causal structure of a training iteration becomes an exponential family over DAG paths whose marginals drive a convex and efficiently differentiable cost; and the decision to reconfigure becomes a likelihood-ratio test whose consequences are quantified by a regret bound. The same language accommodates clusters shared by several jobs, where convex fairness is available precisely in the range α ≥ 2. The experiments indicate both the value and the limits of this view. The continuous the-ory predicts the achievable iteration time accurately once pods have several optical ports, and likelihood-ratio detection gives the best reconfiguration policy among those examined; exact inte-ger programming, however, remains preferable for small instances. Extending the framework to multi-hop routing and contention, and validating it on production traces and optical hardware, are the natural next steps.
