Abstract —Distributed training of sparsely activated mixture-of-experts (MoE) models is often bottlenecked by workload im-balance and all-to-all (A2A) communication induced by dynamic token routing. Existing token deduplication, expert placement, and replication techniques alleviate these bottlenecks but do not jointly capture their distinct load semantics. Under hierarchical token deduplication, communication counts each token once per destination group at each hierarchy level, whereas expert computation processes every token–expert assignment. With replication, both costs depend jointly on physical layout and token-to-copy mapping, making separate optimization subopti-mal. We present PlaceMoE to address this coupling. First, we formulate the joint optimization of expert layout and source-aware token-to-copy mapping for E experts distributed across P

devices, accounting for both hierarchical communication traffic and expert-compute load. Second, we develop a solver that combines co-selection-aware expert grouping, bounded dynamic programming for replica allocation, and alternating refinement of layout and mapping. Third, PlaceMoE overlaps replica-gradient synchronization with attention backward. We evaluate PlaceMoE on a 32-GPU cluster and a 64-NPU cluster using Qwen3-VL and DeepSeek-V3. Experimental results show that PlaceMoE achieves up to a 6.94 × speedup in A2A communication and 1.74 –2.33 ×

speedups in end-to-end training over the state-of-the-art training framework VeOmni. 

Index Terms —mixture-of-experts training, expert replication and placement, hierarchical all-to-all communication 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models scale neural-network ca-pacity through sparse activation: a router selects only a small set of top-k experts for each token, allowing the parameter count to grow without proportionally increasing the compu-tation per token [1]–[3]. In large-scale training, these experts are commonly placed across GPUs using expert parallelism (EP) [2]. In the MoE layer of EP, the experts are activated according to the routing function (which is also a small learnable network) and the token dispatching is typically implemented through an all-to-all (A2A) operation [2,4]. After expert computation from the dispatched tokens, the MoE layer then combines the outputs to the token sources through another A2A [4,5]. This mechanism, however, easily causes load im-balance in both communication and computation [4,6,7]. That is, some experts (also called hot experts ) receive many tokens

to compute, while the other experts require only several tokens according to the routing function. The load imbalance limits the scalability of distributed training of MoE models [6]–[10]. Prior work addresses this imbalance through algorithmic or system approaches. Algorithmic approaches, including GShard [2], Switch [3], BASE [11], and others [12,13], add auxiliary load-balancing objectives to router training. Because these objectives modify the next-token training loss, they may affect model generalization [14,15]. System approaches instead reduce A2A traffic, schedule A2A communication, or overlap it with expert computation [6,8,9,16]–[25]. Among the approaches most closely related to this work, token deduplication [10,26], expert swapping or placement [9, 15,23,26]–[28], and expert replication [7] constitute three ma-jor techniques for mitigating load imbalance in MoE training. Nevertheless, each of these techniques has important limita-tions. First, expert swapping requires runtime state migration and online planning, whose overhead can offset the benefit of improved load balance [9,26]. Second, expert replication adds gradient communication because physical copies must aggre-gate their gradients before each optimizer update [28]. This synchronization can reduce the benefit of improved compute balance. Third, token deduplication gives communication and expert computation different load semantics. Optimizing either cost in isolation may therefore lead to suboptimal end-to-end performance. In this work, we propose PlaceMoE, a load-balanced train-ing approach for MoE models that combines the benefits of token deduplication, expert placement, and expert replication. Existing hierarchical token-deduplication methods, such as HierMoE [26], transfer only one copy of a token when that token is routed to multiple experts located within the same hardware hierarchy, such as a single GPU or multiple GPUs within the same node. The effectiveness of this deduplication depends on the physical expert layout, denoted by L. With ex-pert replication, it also depends on the token-to-copy mapping 

M : each token must be dispatched to an appropriate physical copy while avoiding redundant communication and preserving balanced expert computation. The central problem is therefore: 

Given P GPUs participating in expert parallelism, how can we jointly determine a physical expert layout L and a source-aware token-to-copy mapping M such that redundant token arXiv:2610.14798v1 [cs.DC] 10 Oct 2026 transfers during all-to-all communication are eliminated, each token–expert assignment is executed exactly once, and both communication and computation workloads are balanced? 

Jointly optimizing L and M is challenging. L determines the number and locations of physical copies, whereas M

selects one available copy for each source rank and requested expert. Exhaustive search is combinatorial, and evaluating each pair requires recovering every token’s destination groups at each hierarchy level and the non-deduplicated load on every rank. Pairwise relocation or hot-expert replication reduces the search space but cannot directly coordinate groups of fre-quently co-selected experts and may require a long sequence of decisions to reach a satisfactory solution. We therefore require a structured search strategy that preserves expert co-selection affinity while preventing excessive computational imbalance. PlaceMoE profiles token-level routes and calibrates a joint communication-and-computation cost model. It uses expert demand and co-selection affinity to generate replica allocations and hierarchy-aware layouts, and then refines the source-aware token-to-copy mapping to balance communication reuse against expert-compute load. The optimizer alternates between 

L and M and evaluates the generated pairs on the profiled routes. It can be rerun periodically as routing patterns evolve. At runtime, M is implemented as a lookup table and can be refreshed without moving expert states. Updating L also relocates expert and optimizer states and is therefore per-formed less frequently. PlaceMoE overlaps replica-gradient synchronization with same-layer attention backward. We evaluate PlaceMoE on 32-GPU and 64-NPU clus-ters using Qwen3-VL [29] and DeepSeek-V3 [10] with ShareGPT4V [30] and Tulu-3 [31]. Compared with the orig-inal VeOmni system [32], PlaceMoE achieves up to a 6.94 ×

speedup in A2A communication and 1.74 –2.33 × speedups in end-to-end training. A. MoE Layers and Expert Parallelism a) MoE layers.: A sparse MoE layer consists of a router and E logical experts [1]–[3]. For each input token t, the router selects a top-k expert set Et. The selected experts process the token independently, and their outputs are combined using the router weights. We call each token–expert assignment an 

assigned token ; hence, a routing snapshot with |T | tokens contains |T | k assigned tokens. We preserve the logical top-k

routes and optimize only where they are physically executed. 

b) Expert parallelism.: Expert parallelism (EP) partitions experts across P ranks [4,5]. As illustrated in Fig. 1, an MoE layer uses a dispatch A2A to send tokens to the ranks hosting their selected experts. After local expert computation, a combine A2A returns the outputs to the source ranks for weighted aggregation. Backpropagation performs the corre-sponding communication and computation in reverse. Because these phases are synchronous, the busiest communication group or compute rank determines the layer’s critical path. Conventional EP places one physical copy of each logical expert. With expert replication, a logical expert may have multiple physical copies, and each assigned token must be mapped to one of them. This source-aware token-to-copy mapping determines both the communication groups visited by each token and the assigned-token load of each rank. Logical routing, physical placement, and token-to-copy mapping are therefore distinct but coupled decisions.

A. System Overview 

PlaceMoE operates in three stages: profiling and calibration, layout and mapping optimization, and training execution. It first profiles a short training window under the default or current layout and collects a set of token-level routing snapshots D. Topology-aware A2A microbenchmarks calibrate the communication model, while measured expert-compute times calibrate the computation model. The calibrated models are reused under the same hardware, model, and parallel configuration, while routing profiles can be refreshed during training. Given D, the replica budget, and the physical topology, the optimizer generates candidate replica allocations. For each allocation, it constructs both item-level and group-coherent candidates, hierarchically places physical copies across nodes and ranks, and optimizes M over the copies provided by L. It alternates between the two decisions for a bounded number of rounds and selects the generated pair with the lowest calibrated cost on the profiled routes. At a training-step boundary, PlaceMoE installs the selected 

L and implements M as a lookup table for token dispatch. Updating M only replaces this table, whereas updating L

also relocates expert parameters and optimizer states. Conse-quently, M can be refreshed more frequently than L. During backpropagation, PlaceMoE aggregates gradients across phys-ical copies and overlaps this communication with same-layer attention backward.

D. Training Execution and Replica-Gradient Synchronization Overlap 

After installing L and M , forward dispatch directly looks up M (r, e ) to select the physical copy for tokens from source rank r assigned to expert e.

Hot layout and mapping updates. At a configurable interval, PlaceMoE profiles a token-level routing snapshot and asynchronously launches the L, M optimizer in a separate CPU process. Training continues with the current pair while the optimizer derives the next one. A mapping-only update takes effect by replacing the lookup table at a training-step boundary. For a layout update, the runtime compares the old and new layouts across all MoE layers and groups the relocated expert parameters and optimizer states by source– destination rank pair. Each group is packed into contiguous buffers and transferred through batched P2P communication. Once all destination states are ready, the runtime atomically switches L and M . Thus, training continues without restart, and dispatch never observes a mapping before its expert states are available.

A. Experimental Setup 

Testbeds. Table II summarizes our GPU and NPU testbeds, which use NCCL and HCCL as their respective collective communication backends. 

Models and workloads. We evaluate Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B, which has 48 MoE layers, 128 experts per layer, and top-8 routing, on ShareGPT4V for multimodal training and Tulu-3 for text-only training. Following HierMoE’s scaled-model setup [26], we retain 6 DeepSeek-V3 MoE layers, each with 256 experts and top-8 routing, and train them on Tulu-3. 

Baselines. We compare PlaceMoE with 4 baselines. VeOmni v0.1.11 is the state-of-the-art open-source training framework [32] and uses conventional A2A for token dispatch and combination. Replication maintains 2 fixed copies of each logical expert, arranged as 2 EP16 groups on 32 devices and 2 EP32 groups on 64 devices. Copies follow a default expert order. HierMoE applies hierarchical token deduplication and pairwise expert swaps [26]. EPLB places experts according to non-deduplicated per-expert assignment load and represents the expert-parallel load balancer used by DeepSeek-V3 [10]. 

PlaceMoE jointly optimizes replica allocation, hierarchical expert placement, and token-to-copy mapping. Replication, EPLB, HierMoE, and PlaceMoE share the same hierarchical token-deduplicated A2A runtime, providing a common com-munication substrate; VeOmni retains its original A2A path. Unless otherwise stated, Replication, EPLB, and PlaceMoE use the same 1-fold redundancy budget B = E, adding one copy per logical expert on average. VeOmni and HierMoE use no additional copies ( B = 0 ). 

Methodology. We set Kalloc = 64 , use 3 partition restarts and 3 layout–mapping rounds, and limit capacity assignment to 12 iterations. Pair refinement allows at most 24 exchanges, reduced to 12 for rank-level partitions, while each mapping update performs at most 6 sweeps. All procedures terminate early upon convergence. We discard the first 10 warm-up steps and report the mean over the next 10 steady-state steps. Unless otherwise stated, EP32 and EP64 runs use global batch sizes of 128 and 256, respectively, and each rank processes approximately 16K input tokens. All methods use the same model, data, numerical precision, and optimizer configuration. End-to-end time is the mean wall-clock time per steady-state training step; initial profiling, calibration, and layout–mapping optimization are excluded. A2A time sums the forward and backward critical-rank A2A spans; expert-compute time is measured analogously. All speedups are relative to VeOmni on the same platform and workload.

EPLB’s load-driven layout creates an irregular synchronization graph and leaves this communication on the critical path. Optimizing either objective alone is therefore insuffi-cient: HierMoE reduces communication but can slow expert computation, while EPLB balances computation without ex-ploiting token-level deduplication in placement. PlaceMoE im-proves both components over VeOmni in every configuration and consistently achieves the best end-to-end performance, validating their joint optimization.

In this paper, we present PlaceMoE, an end-to-end MoE training system that jointly optimizes replica allocation, hi-erarchical expert placement, and source-aware token-to-copy mapping. Its calibrated objective captures both duplicate-free hierarchical communication and non-deduplicated expert-compute load, while affinity-aware hierarchical partitioning and alternating layout–mapping refinement make the coupled optimization tractable. At runtime, PlaceMoE asynchronously refreshes the layout and mapping without restarting training and overlaps replica-gradient synchronization with attention backward. Evaluations on a 32-GPU cluster and a 64-NPU cluster using Qwen3-VL and DeepSeek-V3 show that Place-MoE accelerates A2A communication by up to 6.94 × and end-to-end training by 1.74 –2.33 × over VeOmni, demonstrating consistent benefits across hardware platforms and workloads. The component breakdown confirms that optimizing commu-nication or computation alone leaves complementary bottle-necks, whereas their joint optimization consistently improves end-to-end performance. The sensitivity study further exposes a tunable performance–memory tradeoff, and the 600-step experiment shows that asynchronous reoptimization preserves the training benefit with limited exposed update overhead.
