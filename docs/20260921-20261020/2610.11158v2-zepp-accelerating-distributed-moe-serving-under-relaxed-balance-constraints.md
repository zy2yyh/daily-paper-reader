## 摘要
随着混合专家（MoE）模型持续扩展，其服务越来越依赖于跨越来越多设备的专家并行（EP）。然而，偏斜的专家工作负载会在计算、通信和内存方面造成不均衡，使负载均衡成为分布式 MoE 服务中的核心优化目标。我们观察到，均衡并非没有代价：为平衡某一维度而引入的操作本身也可能开销高昂或造成不均衡。这促使我们重新思考，将均衡视为一种约束而非优化目标。我们提出 Zepp，它在对物理资源（即 GPU 和 NIC）施加简化均衡约束的前提下，直接优化分布式 MoE 服务中的瓶颈通信。Zepp 从放置、路由和执行三个层面逐步优化节点间通信。它首先在 GPU 约束下放置专家副本以减少 token 通信，然后在 NIC 约束下通过拆分与合并原语重塑通信流，最后对专家计算进行划分与调度，以与由此产生的通信重叠。为适应动态工作负载，Zepp 在每次迭代中协同协调计算、token 通信和专家权重移动。这些设计共同使 Zepp 能够追求最高效的执行，而非单一维度上的均衡执行。我们实现了 Zepp，并将其与 7 个最先进的 MoE 服务系统进行对比评估，相比最快的竞争基线，最高实现 6.68× 的 MoE 层加速，几何平均加速为 1.86×。

## Abstract
As Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models continue to scale, serving them increasingly relies on expert parallelism (EP) across a growing number of devices. Yet skewed expert workloads create imbalance across computation, communication, and memory, making load balancing a central optimization objective in distributed MoE serving. We observe that balance is not free: operations introduced to balance one dimension can themselves be expensive or imbalanced. This motivates us to rethink balance as a constraint rather than an optimization objective. We present Zepp, which directly optimizes the bottleneck communication in distributed MoE serving subject to simplified balance constraints on physical resources, i.e., GPUs and NICs. Zepp progressively optimizes inter-node communication across placement, routing, and execution. It first places expert replicas to reduce token communication under GPU constraints, then reshapes communication flows through split and merge primitives under NIC constraints, and finally partitions and schedules ex- pert computation to overlap the resulting communication. To adapt to dynamic workloads, Zepp jointly coordinates computation, token communication, and expert-weight movement at each iteration. Together, these designs allow Zepp to pursue the most efficient execution rather than a single-dimension balanced one. We implement Zepp and evaluate it against 7 state-of-the-art MoE serving systems, achieving up to 6.68$\times$ MoE layer speedup and a geometric mean speedup of 1.86$\times$ over the fastest competing baseline.
